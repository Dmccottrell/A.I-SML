# Names: a first, informal check (Oct 6 2026)

**This is not a trademark search and not legal advice.** It is a quick look at what a web search shows, so you know what to ask a trademark attorney or check properly before you make the names public. A real clearance means searching the official registers (USPTO's trademark search, WIPO's Global Brand Database, EUIPO, UK IPO, and whichever countries you will sell in) for the names and for similar-sounding and similar-looking ones, in the right classes (software, AI services).

## What this means for how you use them

- The five model names are ordinary words that others already use around AI. A name like **"Emvryx Flare"** (always with the Emvryx stem, as the app and site show it) is far safer than "Flare" or "Apogee" on their own. Keep using the full form in public.
- Do not register or promote the single words as your own brand. If one ever needs to stand alone, pick a more distinctive word.
- The strongest asset is **Emvryx** (and the logo). Consider registering it (word mark, plus the logo) in the classes for software (9) and AI/online services (42) where you operate, after a proper search.

## Things only you can do

1. Search the official registers for EMVRYX and the sound-alikes (EMRYX, EMVRIX, EMVYRX...). Take screenshots with dates.
2. Check domain names and handles (emvryx.app is in use; also .com, and the main social names).
3. Talk to a trademark attorney before launching publicly or spending money on branding. Many offer a fixed-fee clearance.
4. Keep the open-model credits: the models built on the open base are credited in `NOTICE` and labelled as built on it. If you ever use a name or logo belonging to a model's makers, check their brand rules first.
