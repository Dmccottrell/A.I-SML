ABOUT EMVRYX

Built from the ground up.

Emvryx is one family of four models, built and tuned together here: Flare, Equinox, Solstice and Apogee. The newest generation is partly built on open foundation models (credited in our notices) and then taught Emvryx’s own lessons: looking things up and showing the source, saying “I don’t know”, following your rules. None of ChatGPT’s, Claude’s or Gemini’s answers are in its lessons. Some practice lessons are written by an open model and checked by rules.

Every number here is measured; a dash means not measured yet. HellaSwag is scored on the full 10,042-question test for Flare 2.5 and Equinox 2, and on a 2,000-question sample (about 1 point of wobble) for Solstice 2, each next to the plain base model it started from: tuning here kept the base’s knowledge intact (Flare 2.5 is level with its base, Equinox 2 is about 4 points above and Solstice 2 about 2 points above). The identity test checks that each model says it is Emvryx. The 200-question tests have about 3 to 4 points of wobble, so only big gaps mean something: the clear one is maths word problems on Flare 2.5 (double its base). The rules sheet checks Emvryx’s own way of answering, so plain bases score low on it by design. Equinox 2 is measured on the public tests too (*the plain base scores about 0% on maths, most likely a measuring problem (it starts with long hidden reasoning), so that comparison is not reliable; Equinox 2 itself solves 76.5%). The coding tests use only 50 questions each, so they wobble by about 5 to 7 points. *The plain bases score 0% on MBPP, again most likely a measuring problem. HumanEval for Flare 2.5 and Equinox 2 is being re-run after a fix, and the VryxCode row is only 10 exercises, so treat it as a rough guide (for comparison, an outside specialist coding model solved 8 of 10 on the first try and 9 after a retry). Flare 2.5 does not run VryxCode.

Its own brain

We wrote the tokenizer, the transformer and the training loop, then trained it on carefully chosen reading: educational pages, everyday web text, all of English Wikipedia, code in many languages and step-by-step maths.

Taught like a good assistant

It answers from its notes and names the source. It says “I don’t know” instead of guessing, rechecks when you say it’s wrong, keeps to exact counts and formats, follows a change of topic, and reads through typos.

A family that keeps improving

Every new version keeps everything the last one learned, and must beat it on the same tests before it replaces it. Bigger siblings follow, and the app stays the same.

At home or in the cloud. Private or public.

Any Emvryx model can run wherever you want it, and be shared as widely as you want. Same models, same app; only the address and the guest list change.

WHO’S BEHIND EMVRYX
DM

Darryl McCottrell

Computer Science major · Core Engineer at Impact Networking

I love games, anime and technology, and most of all how AI is put together: the architecture and the infrastructure behind it. I use tools from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Gemini, and each one had something I wished the others had. So I decided to make my own.

Emvryx is my personal family of small language models. Some I built and trained from scratch; others start from open models released under the Apache 2.0 licence, which I then trained with Emvryx’s own lessons. I wanted something personal that matches my other projects and works with them, and that saves money for apps that would otherwise pay for an AI service. Claude will always be on top for me, but Emvryx is mine.

I hope Emvryx inspires you not to limit yourself. Choose to create, do what you want to do, and rise above it.

What to keep in mind

Like any AI, Emvryx can make mistakes. That is why it shows its sources and which model answered, so you can check what matters, especially for homework, health and money.
